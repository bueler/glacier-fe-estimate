Title: When a glacier evolves by Stokes flow, is it minimizing something?

Author: Ed Bueler, University of Alaska Fairbanks

Abstract:  The velocity and pressure fields, in a Stokes stress balance for ice flow, are well-known to be the minimizers of an incompressibility-constrained optimization problem. However, does a similar property hold when the glacier _evolves_, that is, when we couple the Stokes flow to the free-surface (kinematical) equation? Furthermore, is this fluid dynamical system well-posed? On a non-flat bed, at least, the coupled system does not literally minimize an objective, but I will show numerical evidence that a coercivity property approximately applies. Because the mathematical framework for this coupled problem is inequality- and algebraically-constrained, and not well-handled by any fluid-mechanical or numerical theory, coercivity represents a foothold when building a non-shallow theory. The overall goal of this work is to understand the well-posedness of the coupled dynamical system, and to build performant finite element solvers for it, based only on physics (and not learning-with-many-parameters).

Submitted to:  C021. Modeling of the Cryosphere: Glaciers and Ice Sheets
